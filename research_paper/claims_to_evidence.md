## Summary

- **33 of 33 claims** now trace directly to a specific, currently-saved file in `results/` or to code read directly by the author. This makes the paper's own §6 reproducibility claim fully true rather than true-by-careful-scoping.
- **Claims #9, #15, and #27/#28** were reproducible-but-unsaved findings from an earlier pass; each is now a persisted, reusable script under `backend/scripts/` with output saved to `results/` (`benchmark_pc_algorithm.py`, `check_registry_split_date.py`, `diagnose_fold_concentration.py`). Re-running all three reproduced the original findings, with one refinement: the DAG-timing comparison (#9) turned out more severe on re-measurement — the old `chi_square` default did not complete within a 3-minute timeout at all, rather than merely taking "several minutes."
- **Claim #6 was substantively corrected, not just re-verified.** Re-deriving it exposed a real bug: `phase2_causal_evaluation.py`'s `TREATMENTS`/`OUTCOMES` lists were hardcoded to a 5×5 subset (missing `GDP_Change` and `Unemployment_Rate_Change`, and 6 of the 11 sectors) rather than the full 7×11=77 grid the paper's methodology section describes. The `max_pairs=25` parameter was a red herring that happened to equal 5×5 and was never the actual constraint. Both lists were corrected to the full grid, `phase2_ate_results.csv` was regenerated with all 77 pairs, and the BH-FDR correction is now computed inside `run_causal_evaluation()` itself (via `statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests`) rather than as a manual post-hoc CSV edit. The corrected numbers (27 of 77 significant raw, 18 after FDR) are materially different from, and more favorable to the causal-discovery layer than, the old 25-pair numbers (7 raw, 1 after FDR) — this is reflected in the abstract and §4.6, which now also note the resulting disconnect between statistically real treatment effects and the null portfolio-level result.
- **No citation was included without independently confirming it exists** via search — several candidate related-work papers found during search (e.g., a cluster of "Chen, Pelger, & Zhu (2021)"-style papers surfaced only in a search engine's own summary, without a direct source link) were deliberately **excluded** because their existence and bibliographic details could not be independently confirmed from a primary source.
